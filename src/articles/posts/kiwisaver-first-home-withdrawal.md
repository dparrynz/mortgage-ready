For most first home buyers, KiwiSaver is the biggest part of their deposit. A KiwiSaver first home withdrawal lets you take out almost all of your balance to put towards buying a home you'll live in.

The rules are straightforward, but the timing catches people out. Here's what you need to know before you start looking.

## Who can make a first home withdrawal?

You can usually make a first home withdrawal if you:

- have been a KiwiSaver member for **at least 3 years**
- **haven't made a first home withdrawal before**
- **don't already own a home or land** (some previous home owners can still qualify, see below)
- are buying a home or land **to live in as your main home**, not as an investment

There's no limit on the price of the home, and no income cap. Those limits apply to other schemes, such as the [Kāinga Ora First Home Loan](/first-home-playbook/kainga-ora).

## How much can you take out?

You can withdraw all of your KiwiSaver balance except:

- **$1,000**, which must stay in your account
- any money you **transferred from an Australian super fund**, which stays in KiwiSaver until retirement

Your balance includes your own contributions, your employer's contributions, government contributions and investment returns, so it often adds up to more than people expect. Check your latest statement or log in to your provider's website to see your current balance.

If you're buying with a partner, you can each make a withdrawal, as long as you each meet the criteria. Two KiwiSaver balances can make a big difference to a deposit.

## What if you've owned a home before?

You may still qualify as a **previous home owner** if your financial position is similar to a first home buyer's, for example if you no longer own the property and don't have significant assets. This is sometimes called a "second chance" withdrawal. You apply to Kāinga Ora first, and they confirm whether you're eligible before you apply to your KiwiSaver provider.

## How does the withdrawal process work?

1. **Get pre-approval for your home loan.** Your adviser and bank will include your KiwiSaver balance in your deposit. Lenders usually want to see a KiwiSaver eligibility letter from your provider, confirming the exact amount you're eligible to withdraw.
2. **Find a home and sign a sale and purchase agreement.** You can't apply for the withdrawal until you have an agreement for a specific property.
3. **Your lawyer prepares the paperwork.** This includes a letter of undertaking to your KiwiSaver provider, confirming the details of the purchase.
4. **Fill in your provider's first home withdrawal form.** Each provider has its own form, and it usually needs to be signed in front of a lawyer or Justice of the Peace.
5. **Your provider processes the withdrawal.** Allow **10 to 15 working days**.
6. **The money goes to your lawyer's trust account.** Your lawyer then pays it towards your purchase. The money never goes to you directly.

## Deposit or settlement: when is the money used?

You need to decide which payment your KiwiSaver will go towards **before you apply**.

- **The deposit.** This is the payment due when your agreement becomes unconditional. To use KiwiSaver for this, apply as soon as your agreement is signed, and make sure the agreement includes a clause saying the deposit will be held until settlement rather than released to the seller. Your lawyer can add this before you sign. Talk to them first, because adding it afterwards can mean renegotiating with the seller's lawyer.
- **Settlement.** This is the remaining balance, paid on the day the home becomes yours. Most buyers use KiwiSaver here, as the timing is easier. Apply at least 10 to 15 working days before settlement.

**Buying at auction?** KiwiSaver can only go towards settlement, not the deposit, because the deposit is due on auction day. You'll need cash for the deposit.

Our article on [seller deposits and bank deposits](/articles/seller-deposit-vs-bank-deposit) explains why these two payments are different, and why some sellers prefer a cash deposit.

## What if the purchase falls through?

If the purchase doesn't go ahead, your lawyer pays the money back to your KiwiSaver provider. You don't lose your savings, and you can still make a first home withdrawal for another property later.

## Is the First Home Grant still available?

No. The KiwiSaver First Home Grant closed to new applications in May 2024. The first home withdrawal is a separate scheme and is still available.

## Tips to avoid delays

- **Check your eligibility early.** Confirm your 3 year membership date and balance before you start house hunting.
- **Tell your lawyer and agent you're using KiwiSaver** before you sign anything, especially if it's going towards the deposit.
- **Build the processing time into your dates.** Make sure there are at least 10 to 15 working days between going unconditional and settlement.
- **Keep some cash available** for the deposit, in case KiwiSaver can't be used for it.

> **The bottom line:** KiwiSaver can make up most of your first home deposit, but it takes time to release and must be planned around your sale and purchase agreement. Check your eligibility early and let your lawyer know your plans before you sign.
